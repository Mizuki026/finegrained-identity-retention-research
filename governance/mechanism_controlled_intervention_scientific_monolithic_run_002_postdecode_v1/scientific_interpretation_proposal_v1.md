# Controlled-intervention run-002 scientific interpretation proposal v1

Status: proposed for researcher acceptance; not yet an accepted scientific interpretation.

## Verified facts

- All 480 videos, 3,360 sampled frame labels, 120 unit-condition scores, and 20 preregistered pair effects are complete. No reduced panel or imputation was used.
- Eyeglasses primary target-versus-sham effects: 0 correct, 7 ties, 3 wrong; mean -0.0232143, median 0, range [-0.125, 0].
- Facial-hair primary effects: 2 correct, 7 ties, 1 wrong; mean 0.00509370, median 0, range [-0.0292398, 0.0555556].
- No p-values, confidence intervals, cross-attribute pooling, or cross-attribute ranking were computed.
- Eyeglasses valid coverage was 530/560 baseline, 531/560 target, and 533/560 sham. Facial-hair valid coverage was 379/560 baseline, 382/560 target, and 380/560 sham.
- The two blind submissions agreed on 1,521/3,360 states; 1,839 disagreements were adjudicated. Human independence is researcher-attested and cannot be established from the CSV files alone.

## Current fixed-panel observation

The complete-family projection exchange did not produce a predominant predicted target-versus-sham effect. Eyeglasses had no positive pair effect and three negative effects. Facial hair had two modest positive effects, one modest negative effect, and seven ties. The primary pattern is therefore sparse, mixed, and centered near zero rather than a robust direction-specific effect across the fixed panel.

Many unit-condition scores were saturated at role-consistent floors or ceilings. Every eyeglasses control score was zero in all conditions, and many positive scores were one. Facial-hair positive scores were also usually one. This restricts observable score movement and is an important measurement-state limitation, not a basis for changing the frozen metric after seeing results.

The complete secondary diagnostics do not rescue a general directional claim. For eyeglasses FEY_01, the sham-versus-baseline role-aligned effect (0.125) exceeded the target-versus-baseline effect (0.1071), yielding a negative target-versus-sham result. FEY_05 and FEY_07 moved in the wrong target direction while sham was unchanged. For facial hair, FFH_08 and FFH_09 showed modest predicted target-versus-sham effects; FFH_10 moved oppositely, and FFH_04 showed the same small target and sham movement relative to baseline.

## Proposed scientific interpretation

Under this fixed intervention dose, LFE interface, complete 32-token family, and finite panel, the experiment does not support a broad causal contribution of the exchanged held-pair-out directional projection to the generated positive-versus-control attribute contrast. It provides limited pair-specific direction-consistent evidence for facial hair at FFH_08 and FFH_09, counterevidence at FFH_10 and three eyeglasses pairs, and no detectable target-specific difference for most pairs.

This is evidence about the effect of this particular intervention, not evidence that attribute information is absent from LFE or that the local route is irrelevant. Saturated readout, off-manifold displacement, nonlinear downstream use, and compensation or interaction with other paths remain plausible explanations. The accepted working models of representation-readout mismatch/reorganization and multi-path distributed representation remain compatible with the result but are not verified by it.

## Decision recommendation

Close the preregistered intervention analysis as a complete mixed/near-zero fixed-panel result. Do not rerun, change dose, select favorable pairs or tokens, replace the human metric, or introduce a post-hoc frame family. Do not claim natural mediation, bottleneck behavior, route necessity or sufficiency, localization, population/model-wide behavior, or cross-attribute ordering.
